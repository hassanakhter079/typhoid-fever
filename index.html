TYPHOID FEVER / ENTERIC FEVER

SYSTEMIC INFECTION · COMMUNITY MEDICINE + INTERNAL MEDICINE
"Believed to be the biggest killer of the Human Race"

Pathogenesis
Invasion Route
Ingestion → Distal Ileum → M-cells → Peyer's Patches → Macrophage uptake → Salmonella-containing vacuole → Lysosome fusion BLOCKED (T3SS) → Mesenteric lymph nodes → Thoracic duct → Systemic (Liver · Spleen · BM · GB · Lungs · Brain) → Macrophage apoptosis → Bacteremia / Sepsis
Vi CAPSULE
Blocks opsonization → suppresses neutrophil recruitment → induces monocyte/macrophage response instead (Trojan horse strategy)
TYPE III SECRETION SYSTEM (T3SS)
Injects effector proteins → prevents vacuole-lysosome fusion → S. typhi survives and replicates intracellularly
Clinical Progression — Week by Week
1
WEEK OF INVASION
  • Headache, Malaise
  • High fever >101°F (step-ladder pattern)
  • Abdominal pain
  • N/V/Anorexia (adults)
  • Constipation (adults) / Diarrhea (children)
  • Mild non-specific cough/bronchitis (~⅓)
  • White-coated tongue
  • Relative bradycardia (Faget sign)
2
WEEK OF ADVANCEMENT
  • Fever persists + worsens
  • GIT symptoms advance
  • Splenomegaly
  • Rose spots (40%) — red eruptions on trunk, visible in evening with fever
  • Hepatomegaly may appear
3
WEEK OF COMPLICATIONS
  • LOCAL:
  • GIT hemorrhage
  • Intestinal perforation
  • Peritonitis
  • SYSTEMIC:
  • Hepatitis · Cholecystitis
  • Myocarditis · Pneumonia
  • Meningitis · Arthritis
  • Osteomyelitis
  • Septic shock · DIC
4
WEEK OF RECOVERY
  • Fever normalizes
  • GIT symptoms resolve
  • Pulse + ECG normalize
  • 2 possible fates:
  • Relapsing fever (after ~10 days)
  • Chronic carrier state
💡
Relative Bradycardia (Faget Sign): Normally, HR increases ~10 bpm per 1°F rise. In typhoid, myocarditis blunts this response — e.g., at 103°F, HR remains ~92 (expected ~110). This dissociation = relative bradycardia.

⚠️
Widal Test Pitfall: 40% sensitivity + 15% false positive rate makes it unreliable as a standalone diagnostic. Typhi-dot (70%) is superior. Blood culture remains the definitive investigation of choice.
CURE CRITERION: 6 consecutive negative stool/urine cultures
Treatment
Supportive Management
  • IV Fluids (electrolyte replacement)
  • Antipyretics (paracetamol preferred)
  • Antiemetics
  • NSAIDs for pain/fever
  • Hygiene + sanitation counselling
⚠️
Antibiotics can prolong fecal excretion of the pathogen — inform patient.
Antimicrobial Therapy
1ST LINE — Inj. Ceftriaxone
3g IV OD × 14 days
1ST LINE (ALT) — Inj. Ciprofloxacin
750mg IV BD × 14 days
RELAPSING / RESISTANT — Inj. Azithromycin (DOC)
500mg IV BD × 7 days
CURE = 6 consecutive negative stool/urine cultures
Carrier State
Typhoid Carrier Management
DEFINITION
Harbors S. typhi without signs/symptoms. Organism resides in gallbladder → periodically shed via bile into gut → excreted in stool. Can persist for decades.
RESERVOIR ORGAN
Gallbladder (bile provides alkaline pH that S. typhi prefers). Risk ↑ with cholelithiasis.
STEP 1 — Tab. Ciprofloxacin
500mg PO BD × 6 weeks
Then recheck: 6 consecutive stool/urine cultures after ≥6 weeks
STEP 2 — If Antibiotics Fail
Cholecystectomy (DOC)
Surgical removal of gallbladder — eliminates the bacterial reservoir
High-Risk Complications
🩸
Asplenia / Sickle Cell Disease: Loss of splenic immunity against encapsulated organisms → S. typhi → Osteomyelitis (bone infection)
💀
Ileal Perforation → Peritonitis: Macrophage-driven tissue necrosis in Peyer's patches damages bowel wall → perforation → secondary peritonitis (surgical emergency)
⚡
Bacteremia → Sepsis: Macrophage apoptosis releases bacteria into bloodstream → systemic vasodilation, hypoperfusion of vital organs
Prophylaxis (Vaccines)
Vi Polysaccharide Vaccine (IM)
Inactivated · Single dose · Adults >2 years
Contains purified Vi capsular antigen; booster every 2–3 years
Ty21a (Oral Live Attenuated)
4 capsules on alternate days · Adults >6 years
Live vaccine — contraindicated with antibiotics and in immunocompromised
Feco-oral prevention Safe water/food Hand hygiene
High-Yield Pearls
PANCYTOPENIA
All 3 cell lines ↓ in typhoid — differentiates from most other bacterial infections causing leukocytosis
FAGET SIGN
Pulse-temp dissociation. Also seen in: yellow fever, brucellosis, leptospirosis, legionella
ROSE SPOTS
Salmon-colored macules on trunk (chest/abdomen), seen in ~40%, best visible in evenings when fever peaks
INTRACELLULAR SURVIVAL
T3SS blocks vacuole-lysosome fusion → S. typhi replicates inside macrophages → uses them as transport
INVESTIGATION SEQUENCE
Blood cx (Wk1) → Widal/Typhi-dot (Wk2) → Stool cx (Wk3) → Urine cx (Wk4) → BM cx (partial Rx cases)
CARRIER SITE
Gallbladder → bile duct → intestine → feces. Cholelithiasis promotes carrier state. Cholecystectomy = definitive Rx if antibiotics fail
